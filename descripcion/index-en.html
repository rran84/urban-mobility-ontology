Abstract

This document presents the specification of the Shared Mobility Ontology (myOntology), a formal model developed in OWL 2 to represent urban shared mobility services. The ontology covers the operational management of vehicles (urban passenger cars with and without a driver), service points and parking infrastructures, agents, bookings, trips, tariffs, invoices, and payment methods, integrating ontology design patterns and reused SKOS schemes from SEGITTUR and EDINT.

Introduction back to ToC

Urban shared mobility encompasses a variety of transport services that enable vehicle usage without ownership. Given the heterogeneity of operators and service modalities, this ontology provides a homogeneous and interoperable semantic framework. Developed following the NeOn methodology, it formalizes the domain's functional requirements and competency questions, reusing urban standards and controlled vocabularies to facilitate integration into smart city platforms.

Ontología de Movilidad Compartida (myOntology): Description back to ToC

The conceptual structure of the Shared Mobility Ontology (myOntology) is articulated around six main modules: fleet management and shared vehicles, infrastructures and service points, agents and roles, booking management, trip planning with support for multi-stop routes, and the economic domain of tariffs, invoicing, and payments. The model applies standard ontology design patterns and links its properties with proprietary and external SKOS schemes (SEGITTUR and EDINT).

Battery Electric Vehiclec back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: http://schema.mobivoc.org/#BatteryElectricVehicle

Electric vehicle that uses chemical energy stored in rechargeable battery packs. Battery electric vehicles use electric motors and motor controllers instead of internal combustion engines for propulsion. They derive all power from battery packs and thus have no internal combustion engine, fuel cell, or fuel tank.

Business entityc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: http://purl.org/goodrelations/v1#BusinessEntity

An instance of this class represents the legal agent making (or seeking) a particular offering. This can be a legal body or a person. A business entity has at least a primary mailing address and contact details. For this, typical address standards (vCard) and location data (geo, WGS84) can be attached. Note that the location of the business entity is not necessarily the location from which the product or service is being available (e.g. the branch or store). Use gr:Location for stores and branches.
